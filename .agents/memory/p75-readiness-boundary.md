---
name: P7.5 collection readiness boundary
description: Authority limits for the read-only pre-collection readiness report.
---

Keep P7.5 readiness reports and evidence packs diagnostic only. A clean machine report must not start a cohort or authorize collection. Mission IDs and the fixed held-out partition are metadata, not proof of independent sampling or an unused holdout. Discover and evaluate existing trusted sources before considering new authority infrastructure; different evidence classes may need different sources for reviewer identity, operator/reset, sample-selection lineage, held-out provenance, and protocol freeze. Keep `SOURCE`, `AUTHORITY`, and `EVIDENCE` distinct: a source or issuer existing does not establish its authority for a specific claim. Any future binding must identify issuer, authority basis, exact claim, scope, protocol revision, validity time, and invalidation/conflict rules. Keep source verifiers separate from the evidence-pack orchestrator. Episode IDs in a registration payload do not prove ledger ownership: verify the persisted event row, payload hash, actor/correlation, parent Episode identity, and contiguous stream before marking machine provenance verified. That verification proves agreement with the current database snapshot, not independent immutability or human approval. Represent `SERVER_VERIFIED`, `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED`, `MISSING`, `CONFLICTING`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, and `UNVERIFIABLE` separately; a present source with unestablished authority is `UNVERIFIABLE`, not accepted. Absence is not proof of falsehood but must block readiness. Do not map `MISSING` to human review or accept arbitrary caller booleans or unverified strings as reviews.

**Why:** P7.5 records prove technical scope and result integrity but do not by themselves prove durable Episode event ownership, operator-controlled reset, independence, or whether outcome data were previously used for tuning. A database-backed provenance check closes only the machine-record binding gap; it cannot create a trusted human authority or turn hashes into an immutable signature. A single assumed source may not cover distinct trust properties, and source discovery must not silently become a new IAM build. Keeping source existence separate from issuer authority prevents an existing identity provider or database row from being treated as authorization by implication. Documentation/runtime disagreement about states such as `UNVERIFIABLE` can create competing readiness semantics, so reconcile the taxonomy before source selection or verifier implementation.

**How to apply:** Read P7.5 records from the durable Episode ledger in one repeatable-read, read-only transaction; recompute the readiness report from those rows, and bind event IDs/hashes and Episode stream metadata into the internal pack. Inventory existing authority sources by evidence class before choosing integrations; `MISSING`, `CONFLICTING`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, and `UNVERIFIABLE` block readiness, and `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED` needs bound evidence plus a verifiable review mechanism. For the next contract milestone, first align documented states with runtime types and blocker classification; only then record owner decisions about qualifying sources, and implement separate verifiers after those decisions. Trusted-source verification never grants collection authority; any final human approval stays separate. Any future endpoint or UI may surface blockers but must remain read-only until a separate reviewable authority contract exists. Keep `fixed_safe_probe` and calibration v1 unchanged; continuation receipts remain excluded. The pack must remain `BLOCKED` or `REVIEW_REQUIRED` with `collectionAuthorized=false`.
