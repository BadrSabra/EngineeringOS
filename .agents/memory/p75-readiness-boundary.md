---
name: P7.5 collection readiness boundary
description: Authority limits for the read-only pre-collection readiness report.
---

Keep P7.5 readiness reports and evidence packs diagnostic only. A clean machine report must not start a cohort or authorize collection. Mission IDs and the fixed held-out partition are metadata, not proof of independent sampling or an unused holdout. Discover and evaluate existing trusted sources before considering new authority infrastructure; different evidence classes may need different sources for reviewer identity, operator/reset, sample-selection lineage, held-out provenance, and protocol freeze. Keep `SOURCE`, `AUTHORITY`, and `EVIDENCE` distinct: a source or issuer existing does not establish its authority for a specific claim. Any future binding must identify issuer, authority basis, exact claim, scope, protocol revision, validity time, and invalidation/conflict rules. Keep source verifiers separate from the evidence-pack orchestrator. Episode IDs in a registration payload do not prove ledger ownership: verify the persisted event row, payload hash, actor/correlation, parent Episode identity, and contiguous stream before marking machine provenance verified. That verification proves agreement with the current database snapshot, not independent immutability or human approval. Represent `SERVER_VERIFIED`, `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED`, `MISSING`, `CONFLICTING`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, and `UNVERIFIABLE` separately; a present source with unestablished authority is `UNVERIFIABLE`, not accepted. Absence is not proof of falsehood but must block readiness. Do not map `MISSING` to human review or accept arbitrary caller booleans or unverified strings as reviews. Keep owner governance decisions distinct from runtime evidence states: `QUALIFIED_SOURCE`, `NO_QUALIFIED_SOURCE`, `HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REQUIRED`, and `DEFERRED` are decision-record values, not proof states, and must not become hard-coded trusted sources in runtime. A selected source still needs a verifier; no qualified source and deferred decisions remain blocked.

**Why:** P7.5 records prove technical scope and result integrity but do not by themselves prove durable Episode event ownership, operator-controlled reset, independence, or whether outcome data were previously used for tuning. A database-backed provenance check closes only the machine-record binding gap; it cannot create a trusted human authority or turn hashes into an immutable signature. A single assumed source may not cover distinct trust properties, and source discovery must not silently become a new IAM build. Keeping source existence separate from issuer authority prevents an existing identity provider or database row from being treated as authorization by implication. Documentation/runtime disagreement about states such as `UNVERIFIABLE` can create competing readiness semantics, so reconcile the taxonomy before source selection or verifier implementation. Governance decisions and observed evidence answer different questions; collapsing them can make a human choice look like runtime proof or cause code itself to become the authority.

**How to apply:** Read P7.5 records from the durable Episode ledger in one repeatable-read, read-only transaction; recompute the readiness report from those rows, and bind event IDs/hashes and Episode stream metadata into the internal pack. Inventory existing authority sources by evidence class before choosing integrations; `MISSING`, `CONFLICTING`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, and `UNVERIFIABLE` block readiness, and `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED` needs bound evidence plus a verifiable review mechanism. First align documented states with runtime types and blocker classification. Record owner choices in a separate plan/decision record using `QUALIFIED_SOURCE`, `NO_QUALIFIED_SOURCE`, `HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REQUIRED`, or `DEFERRED`; these choices do not prove runtime evidence and must not be hard-coded as source authority. Implement separate verifiers only after those decisions. Trusted-source verification never grants collection authority; any final human approval stays separate. Any future endpoint or UI may surface blockers but must remain read-only until a separate reviewable authority contract exists. Keep `fixed_safe_probe` and calibration v1 unchanged; continuation receipts remain excluded. The pack must remain `BLOCKED` or `REVIEW_REQUIRED` with `collectionAuthorized=false`.

**Recorded owner decision (2026-09-28):** `QUALIFIED_SOURCE` applies to Clerk session identity only; `HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REQUIRED` applies to reviewer approval, reset, protocol freeze, and evaluator applicability; `NO_QUALIFIED_SOURCE` applies to independent sampling and held-out provenance.

**Why:** These decisions separate authenticated identity from reviewer authority and acknowledge that current partition and Mission identifiers do not establish sampling lineage or an unused holdout. The choices remain governance records, not proof or collection approval.

**How to apply:** Preserve this matrix unless the owner explicitly revises it. Do not convert these values into runtime evidence; runtime claims remain `MISSING` until independently verified. Keep collection blocked, keep `fixed_safe_probe`, and do not add a verifier or authority mechanism outside a separately authorized scope.

**Identity-binding rule:** The same authenticated Clerk identity is not proof of the same review. A trusted binding must identify the exact review ID, evidence-pack hash, protocol revision, scope, and validity window, with explicit invalidation and conflict handling. Missing, expired, revoked, conflicting, or out-of-scope bindings all block; missing identity binding must not fall back to `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

**Why:** Authentication establishes the account/session, not which specific review was performed or which pack and protocol it covered. Treating a valid Clerk session as a review would silently broaden identity into authority.

**How to apply:** Keep the identity claim separate from reviewer approval and collection authority. Use the binding tuple and fail-closed outcomes when assessing future P7.5 sources; do not create storage, a verifier, or collection permission without separate authorization.
